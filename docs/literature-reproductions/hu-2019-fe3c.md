## Original equation and numerical reproduction

The original velocity law is

\[
U_s=4.627(\pm0.073)+1.614(\pm0.028)u_p,
\]

with velocities in km/s. Using density in g/cm³ gives pressure in GPa:

\[
P-P_0=\rho_0U_su_p,\qquad
\rho=\rho_0\frac{U_s}{U_s-u_p}.
\]

This maps exactly to the existing `LinearUsUpHugoniot`, including its constrained
mass, momentum and energy jump conditions. No new equation family is needed.
The published **4.627 and 1.614** and both printed error widths are preserved.
The source does not specify a confidence level or coefficient covariance.

Run the repository-only numerical audit:

```bash
uv run python -m scripts.hu_2019_fe3c
```

The [numerical report](../data/hu-2019-fe3c.json) retains these distinct checks:

- On all six source `Us/up` pairs, the published line has **Us RMS
  0.0346843 km/s**. Each vertical residual is within its reported Us error
  width; the maximum ratio is about **0.845**. This is a descriptive comparison,
  not a formal sigma or chi-square test with unknown coordinate covariance.
- An independent **equal vertical Us-weight** fit to all six printed pairs
  gives **c0=4.62819684 km/s, s=1.61907248**, with RMS **0.0313787 km/s**.
  Both coefficients lie within their published widths. This supports
  **conditional parameter agreement**, not recovery of author weighting.
- Its diagnostic standard errors are **0.0710508 km/s and 0.0264846**, from
  `RSS/(6−2) × (AᵀA)⁻¹`. They condition on printed up treated as exact and
  exclude shared systematic and initial-state errors. They are not recovered
  source errors or a claim about the source confidence convention.
- Per-shot Rankine–Hugoniot replay uses each shot's own initial density,
  not the adopted mean. Maximum pressure difference is **0.037303 GPa**;
  maximum density difference is **0.0033734 g/cm³**, smaller than every
  corresponding quoted density width. Source numbers remain unchanged;
  the small density mismatch is not asserted to be explained by rounding alone.

The source and diagnostic curves, coordinate errors, residuals and coefficient
errors appear in the
[native-coordinate reconstruction report](../reconstruction-reports/cementite_nonmagnetic.md#record-cementite_nonmagnetic_hu_2019_linear_hugoniot).
The existing generic Us–up replay reads all six source rows and stored

remain solid.
Residual sign is **Us(model) − Us(input)**, with unweighted descriptive RMS

## Initial state, porosity and volume meaning

The source identifies the precursor as Pnma cementite with **Z=4** by XRD.
Its mean Archimedes density is **7.525(0.010) g/cm³**, compared with a cited
dense ferromagnetic density of 7.687 g/cm³: approximately **2% porosity**.
Individual shot densities span **7.478–7.546 g/cm³** and remain in the dataset.

The installed curve explicitly adopts the reported **mean bulk density** as
its common reference. This is a reference normalization, not a claim that the
authors assigned identical initial density to all six shots or that porosity
was removed. The curve's operational volume for four Fe3C formula units is

\[
V_0=\frac{4M}{\rho_0N_A}10^{24}
=158.481407231785\ \mathrm{\mathring A^3},
\]

using M=179.546 g/mol and the exact Avogadro constant. This is **bulk
mass-equivalent volume including pores**, not a diffraction cell volume.
Its uncertainty is dependent on rho0, so no independent V0 error is invented.
It is not Sata's extrapolated nonmagnetic V0, the existing structural reference
cell, or Hu's separate Grüneisen reference density, 7.982 g/cm³.

The principal loading path starts from the ferromagnetic cementite material
and reaches the source-assigned nonmagnetic branch. Consequently the record
uses `loading_path: principal` and `branch_kind: transformed`, on the existing
`cementite_nonmagnetic` card with `initial_state.material_identifier: cementite`.
The existing Prescher nonmagnetic structure is preserved as independent
structural evidence, not relabelled as in-situ Hu shock diffraction.

## Full experimental range and possible melting

The current record retains **85.6–245 GPa**, the complete Table 1 pressure
range. Both the published velocity fit and the separate diagnostic use **all
six shots**. Agreement with those measurements does not impose a 171.6 GPa
fit limit.

The simple usage caveat is:

> Possible melting at higher pressures; density data do not resolve the phase state.

Section 3.1.2 discusses possible solidus intersection around 175 GPa or above
200 GPa, depending on the compared melting curve. Density data alone cannot
resolve partial melting or definitive phase identity. These limitations qualify
the phase interpretation; they do not invalidate the observed full shock path.
The record therefore represents an **empirical experimental Hugoniot**, with
no assertion that every high-pressure state is solid Pnma cementite or that a
melting boundary has been measured. The existing nonmagnetic material card and
solid structural reference do not establish the full path's phase identity.

The initial 1 November catalog addition used the last observation below the
earliest suggested solidus, 171.6 GPa, as a conservative solid-only restriction.
**That restriction is superseded on 2 November 2026.** It was a catalog
interpretation, not the published fit domain. The record's `scope_history`
retains that decision and its replacement without changing any source number.
The contributor-added `solid_subset` data flag remains unchanged solely as a
historical lower-pressure grouping; it no longer limits evaluation, selects fit
inputs or identifies observed phase states.

Record-level pressure validity is **85.6–245 GPa**. The particle-velocity domain
contains all six reported up values, **1.585–3.286 km/s**, and the pressure
endpoints inverted with the adopted mean rho0; its enclosing bounds are rounded
outwards for stable inclusive evaluation. These are experimental-coverage
bounds, not phase-stability boundaries. The model's pressure at a printed up
can differ slightly from that shot's reported pressure because the fitted line
and common mean initial density are not the row's exact measured Us and initial
density. Neither the observation nor its uncertainty is replaced.

The source's **1190–5530 K** temperatures remain calculated outputs from its
shock-temperature model, not pyrometric measurements or a rectangular thermal
validity range. The **300 K initial-state convention** is unchanged.

## Separate thermal candidate and preserved records

The article also derives gamma0=2.23 and q=0.29 on a **7.982 g/cm³** reference,
using a Dulong–Petit heat capacity, the Sata isotherm and an approximately
0.3 MJ/kg ferromagnetic-to-nonmagnetic transformation energy. These source
values and their provenance remain in the manifest as an **unintegrated
thermal candidate**. They are not inserted into the linear shock path, used
to replace the existing Sata record, or represented as measured temperatures.
An independent thermal addition would need its own exact reference/energy
mapping and source-calculated benchmarks.

The record supplies the material's empirical experimental Hugoniot default;
its **equilibrium default remains the existing Takahashi KBr thermal record**.
All earlier source coefficients, datasets, defaults, structural metadata,
Sata pressure diagnostics, Ono–Mibe diagnostic and completed audits are preserved.
The independent six-shot refit is diagnostic evidence only, not an installed
replacement record. No source data are recalibrated or porosity corrected.
