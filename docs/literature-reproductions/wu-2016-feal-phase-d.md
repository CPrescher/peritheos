# Wu et al. (2016): FeAl-bearing phase D spin transitions

Primary source: X. Wu, Y. Wu, J.-F. Lin, J. Liu, Z. Mao, X. Guo, T. Yoshino, C. McCammon, V. B. Prakapenka, and Y. Xiao, *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* **121**, 6411–6420 (2016), DOI [10.1002/2016JB013209](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JB013209).

## Audit result

- **HOLD — LitCurate source row 891.** The paper reports a measured ambient unit-cell volume of 85.711 Å³ for FeAl-bearing phase D, then fits three physically distinct Birch–Murnaghan branches over 0.0001–37 GPa (Fe2+ HS/Fe3+ HS), 41–64 GPa (Fe2+ LS/Fe3+ HS), and 68–110 GPa (Fe2+ LS/Fe3+ LS). The exact coefficients are only in supporting Table S4. The publisher supplementary download was unavailable during this audit, and the authors state that the underlying data are available on request. Digitizing Figure 3 does not sufficiently constrain the extrapolated zero-pressure volumes and moduli of the two high-pressure branches, so no production record is created.
- **HOLD — LitCurate source row 892.** The paper reports a measured ambient unit-cell volume of 85.201 Å³ for FeAl-free phase D and says its pressure-volume data are in supporting Table S2. The exact BM coefficients are again in unavailable supporting Table S4. A reference volume alone is not an executable EOS.
- **REJECT — LitCurate citation row 893.** The K0 = 253 GPa value is attributed by Wu et al. to the different FeAl-bearing phase-D sample of Chang et al. (2013). It is not a Wu et al. source result and has no complete model identity or reference volume in this candidate row.

The three Fe-bearing spin branches are scientifically genuine rather than pseudo-splits. They remain eligible for later implementation if the publisher SI or author-supplied tables become available.

## Reproduction status

No executable record was added. The primary article and Figure 3 were inspected, and digitization was tested, but the high-pressure branch fits were ill-conditioned with respect to their extrapolated reference states. Preserving the family as a documented hold is more defensible than publishing visually inferred coefficients.
