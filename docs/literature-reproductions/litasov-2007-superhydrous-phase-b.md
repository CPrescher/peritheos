# Litasov (2007) superhydrous phase B

Seven experimental parameterizations from K. D. Litasov, E. Ohtani, S. Ghosh,
Y. Nishihara, A. Suzuki and K. Funakoshi, *Physics of the Earth and Planetary
Interiors* **164**, 142–160 (2007),
[doi:10.1016/j.pepi.2007.06.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pepi.2007.06.003),
are represented by `superhydrous_phase_b_lt`. The A89 high-temperature BM3 fit
is the default. All coefficients remain the published values; independent
refits are diagnostics, not additional production records.

## Evidence and scope

The complete 19-page publication of record was read through the
[author-uploaded article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256823665_Thermal_equation_of_state_of_superhydrous_phase_B_to_27_GPa_and_1373_K).
Equations and critical tables were checked in the rendered pages. The publisher
landing page required a browser challenge. No main-paper supplement or erratum
was located in the inspected article, publisher metadata or DOI relations;
this does not establish that none exists. The local Zotero library returned no
matching title or `superhydrous` entry. No software catalog supplied scientific
coefficients. The main-paper PDF was not downloaded as a local binary, so no
checksum for it is claimed.

The [source inventory](../data/litasov-2007-superhydrous-phase-b-source-audit.json)
preserves Table 1 chemistry, all candidate decisions, Table 7 axial coefficients,
and checksums of the retrieved structure paper and its official erratum.
The [numerical report](../data/litasov-2007-superhydrous-phase-b-reproduction.json)
is generated by `scripts/reproduce_litasov_2007_superhydrous_phase_b.py`.

This is nominally pure Mg10Si3O14(OH)4: 35 atoms per formula unit, Z=2 and
70 atoms in the conventional cell. Table 1 EPMA gives Mg9.87Si2.98H4.33O18
before fixing H4, or Mg9.98Si3.01H4O18 after normalization. Hydrogen is inferred
from the analytical deficit; the authors describe the specimen as stoichiometric
SuB. Minor MgO, Au marker and AgPd capsule peaks are distinct from SuB.
The 30 Table 3 reflections are separately bundled with phase and fluorescence
assignments; they are not fitted as a mixture EOS.

The authors infer LT SuB in Section 4.2 because temperatures never exceeded
1373 K; they do not refine the in-situ structure. This qualification is retained.
The material is separate from the previously withheld **Al-bearing Xu (2024)**
study. No Xu coefficients or computed specimen EOS have been added.

## Structure

The diffraction model is the independent MKM0105 LT structure of
[Koch-Müller et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00269-005-0007-4),
Tables 4–5, p. 359: orthorhombic Pnn2, No. 34, Z=2,
a=14.024(21), b=5.109(4), c=8.733(12) Å.
The [coauthor-hosted publication](https://artscimedia.case.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/186/2016/06/14222658/Koch_Muller_et_al_PCM_2005.pdf)
was read, including the site-multiplicity table. Its ideal fully occupied model
has Mg20Si6O36H8 per cell. It is a structural proxy, not a refinement of the
Litasov specimen; its approximately 625.707 Å³ cell is not rescaled to EOS V0.

The source's **long-a** setting is retained for the structure. Table 2 and Table 3
of Litasov use **long-b**, so their axes and hkl must not be combined without
exchanging a/b. Cell volumes are invariant under this exchange.
The [AMCSD-derived COD 9007379 CIF](https://www.crystallography.net/cod/9007379.cif)
explicitly records the authors' correction of O6 z from the duplicated printed
0.3479 to 0.007. The unchanged archive file is bundled and checksummed.
The [official 2014 erratum](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00269-014-0697-6)
corrects H2 from (0.27,0.18,0.45) to (0.45,0.18,0.27), owing to swapped a/c
axes in the IR experiment. The material applies this later correction even
though the archive CIF still contains the old H2 position. H1 is from refinement;
H2 is an IR-based estimate. Neither has a reported coordinate error. Heavy-atom
coordinate errors and lattice errors are preserved in structure provenance;
the corrected O6 z has no reported error. Si1 and O1 z are wrapped modulo one.

## Observations, pressure scales and domains

`superhydrous-phase-b-litasov-2007-table2.csv` retains all 66 in-situ rows
SB2-004–069, three ambient cell measurements, both pressure columns, Au volumes,
all three lattice parameters, sample volumes and all printed errors. Table 2
explicitly calls these errors estimated standard deviations. Its P2 errors are
the same as P1; one shared uncertainty column preserves that fact. Missing
ambient Au readings and pressure errors remain missing. For numerical fitting,
the three ambient observations are assigned P=0 and T=300 K, the authors'
reference state; these are not separately reported thermometer readings.

Seventeen in-situ rows are at 300 K; adding the three ambient cells gives the
20-row RT diagnostic. Thermal diagnostics use all 69 rows. No point is removed
as an outlier. In-situ coverage is 2.7–26.6 GPa on A89 or 2.7–28.1 GPa on T03,
300–1373 K. The RT maxima are 24.3 and 25.1 GPa. The three recovered volumes
average to exactly 623.34 Å³, reproducing the source's separately printed mean.

The specimen was synthesized at 1373 K for 60 minutes and then followed a
complex unloading/heating path. Six of nine heating cycles lie in the phase's
stability field and three outside it. The tabulated marginal extrema are
measurement coverage, **not a rectangular phase-stability guarantee**.
W97Re3–W75Re25 thermocouple pressure-emf corrections were ignored; no numerical
temperature errors are supplied.

The gold references are Anderson, Isaak and Yamamoto (1989),
[doi:10.1063/1.342969](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.342969), and Tsuchiya (2003),
[doi:10.1029/2003JB002446](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003JB002446).
The latter is a computed **calibrant**, not a computed SuB fit. Source pressures
are retained unchanged. Au a0=4.0786 Å sets the diffraction geometry; 4–5 Au
lines and their cell-volume errors give pressure errors of 0.1–0.3 GPa.

The canonical Anderson equation, evaluated using 4.0786³ Å³ as reference volume,
agrees with P1 to 0.035 GPa RMS and 0.090 GPa maximum, within reported experimental
pressure errors but not exact pointwise printed-value equality. This small
realization/rounding difference is recorded, not used to revise P1. The bundled
Anderson record is a **related** EOS, not an asserted exact pressure-conversion
link. Tsuchiya's exact implementation is not bundled. Calibration metadata thus
remains partially resolved despite complete paired marker observations.

## Equations, alternatives and corrections

All seven records use 300 K BM3 reference curves and conventional-cell V0 in Å³.
The existing native models represent the accepted expressions without new
algorithm code.

| Record suffix | Primary source | V0 (Å³) | K0 (GPa) | K0′ | Published-curve RMS against Table 2 (GPa) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `bm3_a89` | Table 4 RT | 623.38(39) | 138.7(3.0) | 4.9(3) | 0.0751 |
| `bm3_t03` | Table 4 RT | 623.34(4) | 134.7(1.8) | 6.0(3) | 0.0755 |
| `htbm_a89` | Table 4 HTBM | 623.47(37) | 135.8(2.6) | 5.3(2) | 0.1264 |
| `htbm_t03` | Table 4 HTBM | 623.34(4) | 132.7(1.5) | 6.2(2) | 0.1341 |
| `thermal_pressure_a89` | Table 5 | 623.50(36) | 135.3(2.3) | 5.3(2) | 0.1155 |
| `mgd_free_debye_a89` | Table 6 | 623.53(35) | 135.5(2.3) | 5.3(2) | 0.1066 |
| `mgd_elastic_debye_a89` | Table 6 | 623.53(35) | 135.5(2.3) | 5.3(2) | 0.1231 |

Every identifier begins `superhydrous_phase_b_lt_litasov_2007_`.
Equation (1) prints 7/3 twice in the difference of powers, which would produce
zero pressure. The standard BM3 7/3 minus 5/3 is an explicit correction supported
by the named model and Table 2 reproduction. For RT T03, Table 4 takes precedence
over the prose on p. 147, which repeats the HTBM values 132.7 and 6.2 with a
different K0′ error. These conflicts remain visible in record provenance.

For HTBM, Eqs. (2)–(4) give
`K0(T)=K0+dK_dT*(T−300)` and
`V0(T)=V0*exp[a0*(T−300)+a1*(T²−300²)/2]`.
Thus a0 is the **T=0 intercept**, not alpha at 300 K. The configuration explicitly
uses linear absolute-temperature expansivity and its integral. A89 has
a0=3.21(14)×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, a1=1.19(37)×10⁻⁸ K⁻², dK/dT=−0.026(3) GPa/K;
T03 has 3.38(15)×10⁻⁵, 1.26(37)×10⁻⁸, −0.027(3), respectively.

For Table 5, Eq. (6) is
`P=P300+[alpha0*K0+(dK/dT)V*ln(V0/V)]*(T−300)` after the authors omit the
unresolved quadratic term. Alpha0=3.78(19)×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹ and
(dK/dT)V=−0.002(2) GPa/K. `alpha_KT_ref=0.00511434 GPa/K` is derived from
alpha0*K0. Its uncertainty is missing because their covariance is unavailable;
the original alpha0 and error are preserved separately.

For MGD, Eqs. (7)–(10) use `gamma=gamma0*(V/V0)^q` and the Debye energy
difference at T and 300 K. The production normalization uses n=35 atoms/formula
unit and molar formula-unit volume. The independent calculation instead uses
70 atoms and energy per physical cell, checking Z and unit conversion.
Table 6 gives either gamma0=1.18(6), q=1.74(45), theta0=552(130) K, or
gamma0=1.33(5), q=2.03(35), theta0=860 K fixed from elastic velocities.
Using the cited VP=9.23 km/s, VS=5.40 km/s and 618.2 Å³ cell gives 862.47 K,
consistent with the adopted rounded 860 K.

**Equation (9) has an explicitly interpreted sign.** It prints
`theta=theta0*exp[(gamma−gamma0)/q]`; the records use
`theta=theta0*exp[(gamma0−gamma)/q]`, required by
`gamma=−dln(theta)/dln(V)`. There is no located author erratum for this equation.
A free diagnostic refit with this law recovers theta0≈553 K against the published
552 K. The literal-sign refit recovers ≈673 K; both signs can fit the pressure
observations closely, so numerical agreement alone cannot prove the authors'
implementation. The thermodynamic identity and coefficient recovery justify
this disclosed interpretation. Literal-sign fits and wrong-atom-count controls
are preserved in the numerical report. The MGD alternatives carry this
qualification and neither is the default.

The following remain outside the executable bundle:

- Two fixed-K0′=4 fits: Table 4 prints K0=145.2(4) and 149.2(6) GPa but no
  separate V0 for those branches. The free-BM3 V0 is not silently borrowed.
- Eq. (5)'s Anderson–Grüneisen alternative: delta=5.4 lacks a separately complete
  parameter set, and the printed delta−K0′=−0.1 conflicts with 5.4−5.3=+0.1.
- Individual-isotherm fits in Figures 6–7: their coefficients/errors are not
  tabulated; plotted fit outputs are not treated as new primary observations.
- The initial six-parameter thermal-pressure fit: no quadratic coefficient is
  supplied, and the authors discard it.
- Table 7 axial fits: their fictive a³, b³ and c³ are not conventional cell
  volumes. All coefficients are preserved in the source inventory. Table 7's
  a-axis K=125.7(3) implies ±0.3 GPa, whereas prose says 126±3; both are noted.
- Comparators from other publications: they are not source-owned SuB fits.

## Reproduction and checks

The independent script uses NumPy/SciPy expressions and 48-point Gauss–Legendre
Debye integration, without importing a Peritheos EOS evaluator. All free
parameters are fitted together, except the elastic branch's fixed theta0.
The diagnostic objective is unweighted pressure residuals: original weights,
parameter covariance and confidence conventions for fitted coefficients are not
specified. All recovered coefficients lie within two published errors; this is
a numerical comparison, not a claim that the source errors form a statistical
confidence interval. The ledger also records local least-squares standard errors.
No new opt-in refit is warranted by these small differences.

A sensitivity calculation uses fixed effective variances
`sigma_P²+(dP/dV*sigma_V)²` evaluated on each published curve. It assumes zero
P/V covariance, because none is provided, and assigns the ambient pressure
anchors exact pressure only for this sensitivity. It is not presented as the
original weighting scheme. All solver outcomes and coefficient comparisons are
retained in the report.

Tests compare all native pressures and inverse volumes to the independent
formula on the actual source observations, verify reference-temperature
cancellation, checksums, complete data counts, reported coefficient recovery,
MGD sign/normalization controls, corrected sites and symmetry-expanded cell
composition. The source's independent Figure 10/p. 151 density bounds are also reproduced
at 35 GPa: the pressure-scale difference is 0.01845 g/cm³ at 300 K and
0.02602 g/cm³ at 1273 K (source bound 0.03); the A89 model spread is
0.00147 and 0.00100 g/cm³ respectively (source bound 0.02). This diagnostic
uses the source's common 3.30 g/cm³ normalization and is an explicit extrapolation
to the figure's 35 GPa comparison, not added experimental coverage.
The Table 2 and Figure 10 comparisons supply independent scientific checks;
native-versus-reference agreement alone would not establish source fidelity.
